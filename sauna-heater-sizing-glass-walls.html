Sauna heater sizing with glass walls: the +1.2 m³ per m² rule

Heater output tables assume a timber cabin with insulated walls. Glass, tile, stone, glass block and concrete absorb and lose far more heat, so a cabin with a glass front needs more heater than its volume suggests. This note from the drawing office at Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions shows how much more.

The method

  1. Measure the net cabin volume (length × width × height, inside the lining).
  2. Measure the area of every uninsulated surface: glass walls, glass door, tile or stone on masonry, concrete.
  3. Add volume for those surfaces. Harvia, for example, treats each m² of glass or other uninsulated surface as roughly 1.2 m³ of extra cabin volume; Tylö and HUUM use about 1 m³. Solid masonry walls need more.
  4. Choose the heater whose rated volume range covers the corrected (effective) volume, ideally near the middle of the range, and confirm against that heater's own installation manual.

Why not a flat multiplier?

A common shortcut is to multiply the cabin volume by a single factor such as 1.6. That works only while the glass share stays about the same. As soon as the glass area changes, the factor is wrong in one direction or the other. Adding volume per m² keeps the arithmetic visible and checkable.

Worked example

CaseCabin volumeGlass areaCorrectionEffective volume
One glass front14.07 m³8.04 m²8.04 × 1.2 = 9.65 m³23.72 m³
Same, flat ×1.614.07 m³—×1.622.51 m³ (about 5 % lower; usually the same heater)
Two glass fronts14.07 m³12.48 m²12.48 × 1.2 = 14.98 m³29.05 m³
Same, flat ×1.614.07 m³—×1.622.51 m³ (about 23 % low; the cabin is under-heated)

With one glass front, the shortcut lands close enough. With two, the effective factor is about 2.0, and a heater picked from ×1.6 will struggle to reach temperature.

Checks after the heater is chosen

  • Control unit: confirm the controller's switching capacity; larger heaters often need a power extension module.
  • Supply: the larger heater changes the cable size and protective device; agree it with the electrical designer early.
  • Insulated glazing: no heater manufacturer we checked publishes a reduced factor for double glazing. Treat it as glass unless an engineering calculation with the glass supplier's U-value says otherwise.
