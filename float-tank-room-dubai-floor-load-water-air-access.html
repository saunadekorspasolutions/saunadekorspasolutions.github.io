Float tank room in Dubai: floor load, water, air and access

Updated: 4 November 2026

Short answer: a float tank is heavy, salty and warm, so the room around it needs four things before the tank arrives: a floor that carries the filled weight, a water supply and drain close by, ventilation that removes warm humid air, and a route wide enough to bring the tank in. Most float rooms we plan in Dubai and across the UAE are about 2.5 × 3.5 m with a shower inside or next door.

Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions designs and builds wet areas for villas and hotels in the Gulf, and the notes below come from that site work.

What is a float tank?

A float tank, also called a float pod, flotation tank or sensory deprivation tank, is a closed or open tub filled with a shallow, very dense solution of water and Epsom salt (magnesium sulphate). The solution is kept close to skin temperature, so a person lies on the surface without effort. The tank has its own filtration, heating and disinfection unit. We have built float rooms in projects in Iraq and Turkmenistan, together with rasul and onsen rooms.

How heavy is a filled float tank?

Heavy enough to check the slab first. A typical tank holds around 25–30 cm of solution over its floor area, and the salt makes that solution much denser than water. Depending on the model, the filled weight is usually in the range of 800–1,500 kg on a small footprint. These are typical practice values; the maker's datasheet gives the exact filled weight and the point loads, and the structural engineer confirms the slab. In a villa, the ground floor or a basement is usually the easiest place.

What water and drainage does the room need?

ItemWhat we plan
Fill watera cold supply near the tank, sized from the maker's fill time
Draina floor drain in the room and a separate drain point for emptying the tank
Showera shower inside the room or directly next to it, used before and after each float
Floor finisha slip-resistant tile that tolerates salt, falling to the drain
Wall finishwaterproofed and salt-resistant up to at least shower height

Salt is the detail people forget. Every person who leaves the tank carries salt water across the floor, so everything at low level, from door frames to skirting and socket boxes, is chosen to resist it.

Why does a float room need its own ventilation?

Because the solution is warm and the room is small. Warm, humid air condenses on cold surfaces, and in a Gulf building those surfaces include every air-conditioned wall. We plan a dedicated extract above the tank, a fresh-air supply, and in larger centres a dehumidifier, so the room does not depend on the building's cooling to stay dry. Lighting and sockets are rated for wet areas.

How do you get the tank into the building?

Measure the route before ordering. Doors, corridor turns, lifts and stair landings decide which tank fits. Some pods are delivered in one piece, others in sections that are joined in the room. If a one-piece tank is chosen, a door or a glazing panel may have to be left out until it is in.

What else should be decided early?

  1. Where the plant sits: the filtration unit next to the tank or in a cupboard with access for service.
  2. Power: a dedicated supply for the heater and pump, from the maker's datasheet.
  3. Sound: a float room is a quiet room, so it is kept away from plant rooms and gym floors.
  4. Cleaning routine: filter checks and solution tests, written into the handover file.
