Steam room vapour barrier and ceiling slope: a checklist

A steam room runs at 40–50 °C at close to 100 % relative humidity for hours at a time. Every surface of the room is therefore wet on the inside and at a lower vapour pressure on the outside, and the build-up behind the tile has to be designed for that difference. This is the short checklist the site team at Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions uses before tiling starts.

1. Put the barrier on the warm, wet side

  1. The vapour control layer sits directly behind the tile bed, on the room side of the substrate. A barrier on the cold side traps moisture inside the wall.
  2. Use a bonded sheet or liquid system that its manufacturer rates for continuous steam exposure, not a general shower membrane. In tile-membrane ranges such as Schlüter's, the steam-room product is a separate, lower-permeance grade.
  3. Cover walls, ceiling and floor as one continuous envelope. The ceiling is the surface most often left out, and it is the one that receives the most vapour.
  4. If the room backs onto a cold or air-conditioned space, check the whole build-up for interstitial condensation with the BS EN ISO 13788 method before fixing the layer order.

2. Treat every joint and opening

  1. Lap and seal sheet joints with the system's own sealing band and adhesive; mixing brands breaks the rated permeance.
  2. Pre-formed inside and outside corners at every wall-to-wall, wall-to-ceiling and wall-to-floor junction.
  3. Sealing collars on the steam outlet, sensor, light fittings, drain and any pipe sleeve. A single open penetration lets vapour into the cavity.
  4. Door frame: membrane turned onto the frame or sealed to it; the door itself has a seal on three sides.

3. Slope the ceiling

  1. Condensate forms first on the ceiling. A flat ceiling lets it collect and drip on bathers; a sloped, vaulted or barrel ceiling carries it to the walls and down to the floor drain.
  2. Take the minimum slope from the membrane or board manufacturer's steam-room detail for the system you are using, and draw it on the section, not on site.
  3. Slope away from seating, towards the wall with the least use.
  4. Check that the ceiling height left after the slope still clears the door head and the light fittings.

4. Floor, outlet and air

  1. Floor falls to the drain, membrane continuous with the walls, drain with a bonded membrane flange.
  2. Steam outlet low on the wall, away from seats and feet, with the sensor on the opposite side at head height so the reading is representative.
  3. Generator sized on room volume and on how much of the room is heavy material (tile on concrete or stone heats up slower than tile on foam board). Size it with the generator manufacturer's method.
  4. Planned air change between sessions so the room dries; follow the psychrometric approach in the ASHRAE Handbook — Fundamentals when the room sits inside a conditioned building.
