Spa Contractor or Spa Developer: Who Owns Which Interface

Updated: 29 October 2026

Short answer: most spa delays come from the 10–12 interfaces where the developer's building and the spa contractor's rooms meet: the slab, the services, the waterproofing, the ceilings and the plant. Agree in writing who owns each one before the contract is signed. A single sauna takes about 2 weeks on site; large spa projects take up to 6 months, and the interfaces decide which end of that range you reach. Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions agrees this split in writing with the developer before the spa drawings are frozen.

What does the developer usually provide?

The shell and the services up to an agreed point. In a typical hotel or residential project the developer's main contractor delivers 4 things: the structural slab with set-downs at the right levels, walls and openings, power, water and drainage brought to marked points, and ventilation and cooling to the spa area. The spa contractor builds the rooms from there.

The detail that matters is the word "to". Power "to the sauna" can mean a cable at the wall or a connected heater. Write the end point of each service on the drawing, not only in the scope text.

What does the spa contractor usually own?

Everything inside the thermal rooms and the equipment that makes them work: sauna and steam room construction, hammam stone and heating, the steam generator, heaters and controls, waterproofing inside the wet rooms, benches, doors and the commissioning. On a turnkey contract the spa contractor also coordinates finishes in the spa area.

Where do disputes usually start?

At 3 interfaces more than any other: waterproofing, ceiling voids and drain positions. Waterproofing is laid by one party and pierced by the other. A ceiling void drawn for lighting has no room for a rain head or a steam line. A drain set 300 mm from where the room drawing needed it means breaking a finished screed.

The fix is simple and cheap at concept stage: one coordinated drawing per room, signed by both sides, with the interface points marked. We explain how that split works on our page about the spa contractor and spa developer roles.

What should the contract say about warranty?

Who warrants what, item by item. Our warranty, for example, is written in 3 parts: 24 months on workmanship and manufacturing, 24 months manufacturer's warranty on electrical and mechanical equipment, and 5 years on structure and waterproofing. If the developer lays part of the waterproofing, the contract must say whose warranty covers that layer.

Frequently asked questions

Is a spa developer the same as a spa contractor?

No. The developer owns the building and its programme; the spa contractor builds and equips the spa rooms. Some spa companies, like us, design, manufacture and install, which reduces the number of interfaces but does not remove them.

Who should test the waterproofing?

The party that laid it, with the other party present, before any finish covers it. The test is recorded with photos and a date, and the record goes into the handover file.

Who coordinates the MEP drawings for the spa?

Usually the developer's MEP engineer, using equipment data from the spa contractor. On larger projects a spa consultant checks the drawings against the room requirements before they are issued.

When should the spa contractor be appointed?

Before the MEP design is frozen. Appointed later, the contractor inherits drain, power and ceiling positions that may not suit the rooms, and the redesign costs weeks.

What does a turnkey spa contract cover?

Design, supply, installation and commissioning of the spa rooms, with the interfaces to the building listed. A turnkey contract still needs the interface table, because the shell and services stay with the developer. See our spa contractor and spa developer page.
