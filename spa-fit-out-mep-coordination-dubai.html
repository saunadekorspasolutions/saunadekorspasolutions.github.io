Spa Fit-Out in Dubai: MEP Coordination for Wet Areas

Last updated: 21 October 2026

A spa fit-out in Dubai usually has three parties drawing the same rooms: the interior designer, the MEP contractor and the spa equipment supplier. Problems rarely come from any one of them. They come from the gaps between their drawings. These are the seven points Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions checks with the MEP team before tiles arrive on site.

1. Slab drops and floor build-up

A steam room or hammam floor needs falls to a drain, tanking and a tile bed. That build-up is often 80 to 120 mm thicker than the corridor. Either the slab is dropped, or the corridor gets a ramp. Agree on it before the structural drawings are frozen.

2. Drain positions per room

Each wet room needs its own floor drain, placed where the falls work and not under a bench support. The steam generator and the sauna both need a drain point in the technical room as well, for the blow-down and for cleaning.

3. Steam pipe route and length

Steam lines should be as short as possible, insulated, and laid to fall back towards the generator or to a drain. A long, flat steam pipe collects condensate and spits water from the nozzle. Fix the generator position next to the steam room before the ceiling services are coordinated.

4. Electrical loads and isolators

Sauna heaters, steam generators and plunge chillers are among the largest single loads in a spa. List each with its rated power, phase and isolator position, and confirm that the distribution board has spare ways. A heater that arrives on site before its supply is sized is a common cause of a delayed handover.

5. Ventilation rates per room

Saunas, steam rooms and hammams each need a defined supply and extract. Steam rooms work under slight negative pressure so vapour does not escape into the corridor. Write the rates per room on one sheet that the spa supplier and the MEP contractor both sign.

6. Vapour barrier and ceiling slope

Behind every steam room and hammam wall, a continuous vapour barrier or bonded membrane is needed, and the ceiling should slope so condensate runs to the walls rather than dripping on bathers. The interior designer's ceiling detail and the membrane manufacturer's minimum slope must agree.

7. Commissioning sequence

Commissioning is where the three drawings meet. Fill and test the drains first, then power the heaters and generators, then balance the ventilation with the rooms at working temperature. Hand over with the readings recorded, not with a verbal "it works".
