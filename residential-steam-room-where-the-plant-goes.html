A Residential Steam Room: Where the Plant Goes

Updated: 13 November 2026

Short answer: a home steam room has more plant than the generator alone. There is a control panel, a temperature sensor, an electrical isolator, often a water softener or filter, a drain point for the generator's flush water, an extract fan, and the drivers for the lights. Each one has its own right place: the sensor and the steam outlet inside the room, everything with electronics outside it, and everything that needs service where a technician can reach it without opening a finished wall. Sauna Dekor Spa Solutions places each item on the plan before the walls are boarded.

A steam room runs at about 40–50 °C at close to 100 % relative humidity, so the rule is simple: only what is built for that air goes inside.

Where does the generator sit, in short?

As close to the room as the space allows, within the steam line length the generator maker sets in its installation guide, and at a point from which the line can fall back towards the generator or the room. That choice has its own article; here it is enough to say that the generator sets where the water, drain and power must arrive, so it is placed first and the rest of the plant follows it.

Why does the control panel go outside?

Most owners set the room before they go in: switch on, set temperature, set the timer. A panel beside the door, in the bathroom or dressing area, does that without walking into steam. If an in-room panel is wanted, it must be one the generator maker supplies for use in the steam room itself. A standard wall panel inside the room is one of the most common failures we are called to.

Where do the sensor and the outlet go?

Both are inside the room, and they are placed as a pair. The steam outlet sits low, usually under the bench or near the floor, away from where feet and legs rest. The sensor sits on the opposite side or higher on the wall, at the height the generator maker gives, so it reads the room and not the jet of steam.

Where do the extract fan and the light drivers go?

The extract fan sits outside the room with a duct to outside air, never into a closed ceiling void. The light drivers, transformers or fibre-optic projector go in the same dry service space as the generator if the distance allows, or in a separate dry cupboard. Only the light ends, rated for wet, hot rooms, go into the ceiling of the steam room.

What changes between a villa, an apartment and a hotel?

  • Villa: usually a cupboard or a small utility room next to the steam room. All plant can share it if it has a drain and air.
  • Apartment: space is tight and the building management decides where water and ducts may go. The generator often shares a cupboard with the water heater, which needs a drain tray and separate access.
  • Hotel spa: a central plant room serves several wet rooms. Steam lines get longer, so the plant room is placed early in the design, not after the rooms.

Frequently asked questions

Can all the plant go in one cupboard?

Usually yes, if the cupboard has a drain, some air movement for the generator's heat, and a door wide enough for service.

Is the generator noisy?

It makes some noise when it fills and heats. In a home, keep it away from a bedroom wall, or put a solid door on the cupboard.

Does the sensor need its own cable route?

Yes. It runs from the room to the generator or panel; the route is set before the wall is boarded.

Can the light driver sit above the steam room ceiling?

Only if that void is dry and has an access hatch. Most of the time a dry cupboard is safer.
